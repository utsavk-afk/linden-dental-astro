steps:
  - title: Meet the chair
    body: Kids get a ride up and down in the chair and a look at the tools before anything goes in their mouth.
  - title: Count the teeth
    body: A quick look with a mirror, counted out loud. Many first visits stop here, and that is fine.
  - title: A quick clean
    body: A soft polish and fluoride if they are happy to keep going.
  - title: Parent debrief
    body: Five minutes with you on brushing, snacks and what to watch for next.
faqs:
  - q: When should my child first see a dentist?
    a: Around their first birthday, or six months after the first tooth comes in. Early visits are mostly about getting used to the place.
  - q: Can I stay in the room?
    a: Yes. Most parents sit right next to the chair. Some older kids prefer you in the waiting room, and we follow their lead.
  - q: What are sealants?
    a: A thin coating painted onto the back teeth to keep food out of the grooves. It takes a few minutes per tooth and does not involve any drilling.

Most adults who dread the dentist can trace it back to one bad visit as a kid. We try hard to make sure that visit never happens here.

First visits go at your child's pace. If all they do is ride the chair and count their teeth, that counts as a good visit, and we book the next one.
