I see three or four first visits a week. The kids who do best are not the bravest ones. They are the ones whose parents kept it boring.

## The week before

Read a picture book about the dentist together, once or twice. Let them brush a stuffed animal's teeth. That is enough preparation. More than that and the visit starts to feel like a big deal, which is the opposite of what you want.

## In the car

Keep it short and true. "We're going to count your teeth and see the chair that goes up and down." Kids handle plain facts far better than reassurance. If you say "it won't hurt", the first thing they hear is the word hurt.

## The word to avoid

Shot. Also needle, drill and pull. We have our own words for the tools, like the tooth counter and the water squirt, and we would love you to leave the explaining to us.

## When it does not go to plan

Some kids cry. Some refuse to open their mouth. We have seen it all, and it is fine. A visit where your child sits in the chair, holds the mirror and goes home is still a good first visit. We will book a short follow-up and try again.

## After

Make a fuss of it, but not with sweets. A trip to the park or picking the dinner works well. Next time, they will remember the park.
